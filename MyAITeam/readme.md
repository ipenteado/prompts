# My AI Team

A practical framework for organizing your work, building specialized AI agents, and implementing quality assurance mechanisms for AI-generated deliverables.

This repository accompanies the **My AI Team** article series, exploring how traditional management principles can be applied to working with artificial intelligence agents.

## Project Objective

Demonstrate how professionals can build and manage their own AI teams by applying fundamental management principles:

- Planning and prioritization.
- Work organization.
- Responsibility definition.
- Delegation.
- Performance monitoring.
- Quality assurance.

## Getting Started

Follow these steps to start building your AI team:

### Step 1 — Organize Your Work

Use the **Personal Work Manager** to map your responsibilities, activities, priorities, deadlines, and deliverables.

### Step 2 — Design Your Agents

Use the **AI Agent Architect** to transform your structured activities into specialized AI agents with clearly defined responsibilities.

### Step 3 — Implement Your AI Team

Deploy your agents and establish workflows that allow them to execute tasks independently or collaborate with other agents.

### Step 4 — Implement Quality Assurance

Use the **AI Quality Assurance Agent** to evaluate deliverables, identify errors, and ensure outputs meet predefined quality standards.

### Step 5 — Continuously Improve

Monitor performance, review validation reports, refine agent instructions, and optimize workflows based on actual results.

## Core Principles

### Don't Automate Chaos

Before automating any process, understand what needs to be done, how it should be executed, and what the expected outcome looks like.

### Define Before Delegating

Every AI agent must have a clearly defined responsibility, scope, inputs, outputs, and success criteria.

### Separate Execution from Validation

Whenever possible, the agent responsible for producing a deliverable should not be the only one responsible for validating it.

### Human Accountability Matters

AI agents can execute tasks and support decisions, but ultimate accountability for business outcomes must remain with humans.
